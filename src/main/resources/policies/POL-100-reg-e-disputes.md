## 1. Customer Liability for Unauthorized Transactions

Under Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.6), a consumer's liability for an unauthorized
electronic fund transfer is limited to $50 if the consumer notifies the bank
within two business days of learning of the loss or theft of the access device.
If notification occurs after two business days but within 60 days of the
statement date, liability is capped at $500. Beyond 60 days the consumer may
bear unlimited liability for transfers occurring after the 60-day window.

## 2. Investigation Timeframes

The bank must investigate reported errors promptly. A provisional credit must
be issued within 10 business days of the error notice if the investigation
cannot be completed within that period. The full investigation must conclude
within 45 calendar days, or 90 days for point-of-sale and foreign-initiated
transactions. Findings must be reported to the consumer within three business
days of completion.

## 3. Evidence of Unauthorized Use

A transaction is presumed unauthorized when the cardholder states they did not
initiate it AND there is corroborating evidence such as: device fingerprints
not previously associated with the account, geographic impossibility,
card-testing activity preceding the charge, or an account-takeover pattern
(credential or contact changes shortly before the transaction).
